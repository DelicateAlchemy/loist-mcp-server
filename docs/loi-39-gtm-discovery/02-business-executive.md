# Feasibility Assessment: Loist as "the Linear of Music"
**Role: MBA Business Executive (strategy/corp-dev) — market, competition, business model, verdict inputs**
**Date: 2026-07-07. Method: live web research + industry structure analysis.**

---

## 1. Market structure & sizing

### 1.1 The revenue pool the buyers sit on

- Global music publishing market: **~US$7.9–8.1B revenue in 2025**, growing ~6%/yr toward ~$10.8B by 2030 ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/music-publishing-market); [ReAnIn](https://www.reanin.com/reports/music-publishing-market)).
- Concentration is extreme: **the three majors (Sony Music Publishing, UMPG, Warner Chappell) hold ~64% of revenue; the top five ~70%**. Independents ~22%, production libraries ~6% ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/music-publishing-market); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/272520/market-share-of-the-largest-music-publishers-worldwide/)).
- The specific workflow Loist targets — sync clearance — sits on a small revenue base: **recorded-music sync revenue was ~US$650M globally in 2024 (2.2% of recorded revenue, per IFPI)**; adding the publishing side, total sync money flowing is very roughly $1.2–1.5B/yr ([IFPI via artist.tools](https://www.artist.tools/post/music-sync-licensing); [dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/sync-licensing-market) claims a broader $3.8B "sync licensing market" but that includes libraries/marketplaces). Software spend that this workflow can justify is a thin slice of a thin slice.

### 1.2 Who the companies are

- **Majors (3):** Sony Music Publishing (~1,800 employees globally — [LeadIQ](https://leadiq.com/c/sony-music-publishing/5a1d85f02400002400605799/employee-directory)), UMPG (~1,300 — [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/pic/universal-combined-publications-group-llc/512448509)), Warner Chappell (~925–1,156 — [Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/warner-chappell-music/employees/)). Combined **~4,000–4,300 total employees**, of which the sync/creative, copyright, licensing and A&R functions relevant to a task tool are plausibly 25–40% → **~1,200–1,700 addressable seats across all three majors, globally**.
- **Large independents (~15–25 firms):** Kobalt (~600–700 employees — [Kobalt](https://www.kobaltmusic.com/contact-us/)), Downtown Music Holdings (~600 across all services — [Downtown](https://downtownmusic.com/about/)), Concord (~500 incl. labels/theatricals — [Datanyze](https://www.datanyze.com/companies/concord/33345789)), BMG (~1,000 but mostly recordings), Reservoir (~70–80 — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/data/reservoir-media-global-employee-headcount-2022-2024/)), peermusic (~250–300), Primary Wave, Hipgnosis/Recognition, Round Hill, Position, Big Machine, Wixen, etc. Publishing-workflow seats per firm: **10–150**. Segment total: **~1,000–2,000 seats**.
- **The long tail:** UK MPA represents **~200–300 member companies** ([MPA/LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/music-publishers-association-uk-); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Publishers_Association_(UK))); NMPA membership is of similar order in the US ([NMPA](https://www.nmpa.org/)). Most members are 1–10 person shops. Globally, publishers with **≥5 professional staff** plausibly number **1,000–2,000 companies**; publishers of any size (incl. writer-owned shells) number in the tens of thousands but are not sellable B2B accounts.

### 1.3 What "the biggest 20%" actually means — the honest math

If the sellable universe is ~1,000–2,000 professional publishing companies, the "biggest 20%" is **~200–400 companies**, of which only ~30–50 have more than 20 relevant seats. Bottom-up seat count for the top-20% campaign:

| Segment | Companies | Relevant seats/co | Seats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Majors | 3 | 400–570 | 1,200–1,700 |
| Large indies | ~20 | 30–120 | 1,000–2,000 |
| Mid indies (top-20% remainder) | ~200–350 | 5–20 | 2,000–5,000 |
| **Total addressable seats** | | | **~4,000–9,000** |

At Linear-comparable pricing ($16–26/seat/mo — [Linear pricing](https://linear.app/pricing); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/developer-tools/linear/)), **100% penetration of every addressable seat is ~$1–3M ARR**. That is the ceiling of the thesis as stated. This is a niche vertical tool market, not a venture-scale seat market. Adjacent seats (music supervisors: only **~340–1,500 in the US** — [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/music-supervisor-jobs/demographics/); [Guild of Music Supervisors](https://www.guildofmusicsupervisors.com/)) do not change the order of magnitude.

## 2. Competitive & adjacent landscape

### 2.1 Incumbent rights/royalty ERPs (system-of-record lock-in)
- **Vistex Counterpoint Suite / Music Maestro** — "industry standard for nearly four decades," **400+ music company clients incl. UMG** ([Vistex](https://www.vistex.com/product-suites/counterpoint-suite/); [Music Maestro](https://www.vistex.com/product-suites/counterpoint-suite/music-maestro/)). Owns works/splits/contracts data at exactly the accounts Loist targets.
- **Synchtank** — asset management + sync licensing workflow + IRIS royalty accounting; clients include **Disney, Sony, Warner Chappell Production Music, peermusic, WarnerMedia** ([Synchtank](https://www.synchtank.com/solutions/music-publishers); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/synchtanks-iris-has-already-processed-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars-now-its-being-made-available-to-music-publishers-large-and-small/)). Subscription, config-priced. **This is the closest direct competitor — it already sells "licensing workflow" to the exact top-20% list.**
- **Curve Royalties, Reprtoir, Rightsline/FilmTrack, Songspace (Anthem), Mediarc** — royalty/catalog workspaces for indies; Reprtoir prices by catalog size, not seats ([Reprtoir](https://www.reprtoir.com/pricing)).

### 2.2 Pitching/delivery layer (owns the supervisor relationship)
- **DISCO.ac** — claims **"trusted by 90% of music supervisors"**; $10–30/user/mo + add-ons ([DISCO](https://www.disco.ac/pricing); [DropCue](https://dropcue.app/disco-pricing)). Owns the pitch/delivery rail between publishers and supervisors — the network Loist would need for two-sided clearance workflows.
- **Source Audio**, Bridge.audio — same layer.

### 2.3 Generic tools (the real incumbent)
Sync and copyright teams today run on **email + Excel + Airtable/Monday + the ERP**. Airtable at $20–45/seat with flexible schemas is the true head-to-head competitor for a "task layer," and switching cost from a free-form Airtable base a sync manager built herself is high in habit terms even if low in dollars.

### 2.4 Where the white space actually is
Nobody owns the **cross-company state machine of a clearance** (request → quote → approval across co-publishers → license) — today it lives in email threads. LOI-39's clearance_tasks lifecycle maps to a genuine gap. But the gap persists partly because: (a) each publisher only sees their side, (b) counterparties won't adopt a small vendor's portal, and (c) Synchtank/DISCO are both one product decision away from shipping it to an installed base.

## 3. Business model

- **Pricing reality:** per-seat at Linear-like prices caps the business (see 1.3). Realistic model is **per-catalog/platform fee**: $6–15k ACV for mid indies, $25–50k for large indies (vertical SaaS median ACV is $25–50k — [Optifai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/b2b-saas-acv-benchmark/)), $100k+ for a major division. Per-clearance transaction pricing is attractive on paper but requires being in the money flow, which requires trust Loist doesn't have.
- **Sales cycle:** deals >$100k with 1,000+ employee companies run **6–9+ months** (120–210+ days), with security review, legal, procurement, 6.8 stakeholders average ([Optifai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/sales-cycle-length-benchmark/); [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-sales-cycle-length-benchmarks-2026-by-acv-vertical)). Splits and contract terms are among the most commercially sensitive data publishers hold; a vendor with **no auth, no multi-tenancy, no SOC 2** cannot begin a major-publisher security review — that's 12–18 months of product and compliance work before the first enterprise dollar.
- **Cold start / two-sided dynamics:** clearance is inherently multi-party (publisher ↔ supervisor ↔ co-publishers ↔ label for the master side). If value requires counterparties on the platform, adoption stalls. The design constraint: **the tool must deliver single-player value (internal pipeline visibility, split-confirmation tracking, deadline SLAs) with counterparties participating via email/magic links**, network features optional.

## 4. Feasibility verdict inputs

### 24-month revenue scenarios (assumes team of 1–3, no enterprise sales hire)
- **Conservative:** 4–6 indie/sync-agency design partners at $5–10k ACV → **~$40–70k ARR**; majors untouched.
- **Base:** 12–18 indie publishers + 2–3 large indies at ~$25k → **~$200–400k ARR**; one major running an unpaid pilot.
- **Optimistic:** ~30 paying accounts incl. one major division at ~$100k and a DISCO/Synchtank integration driving inbound → **~$0.9–1.5M ARR**. Even this best case is a lifestyle-scale SaaS, not "Linear of music."

### Top 3 business risks
1. **Structurally tiny TAM:** ~4,000–9,000 addressable seats / ~200–400 sellable companies; theoretical seat-revenue ceiling ~$1–3M ARR. No campaign fixes arithmetic.
2. **Incumbent lock-in + credible fast-followers:** Vistex/Counterpoint owns the system of record at 400+ music companies; Synchtank already sells licensing workflow to Disney/Sony/WCPM; DISCO owns 90% of supervisors. Any traction is copyable by an incumbent with distribution.
3. **Enterprise-readiness and sales-capacity gap:** solo/small team, pre-revenue, single-tenant, no auth vs. 6–9-month procurement cycles and security review over the industry's most sensitive data (splits, contract terms). The seller-buyer mismatch is severe.

### Evidence that would de-risk
- **5+ paid design partners** among independent publishers or sync agencies (not free pilots) within 6 months.
- **Signed LOIs naming a price** from 2–3 top-50 indies.
- **Workflow shadowing data**: documented hours/deal saved vs. Airtable+email in a real sync team.
- **A distribution partnership** (DISCO, Synchtank, or a PRO) that solves the counterparty problem.

## 5. Strategic alternatives (ranked)

1. **MCP/agent integration layer on incumbents (best fit to actual assets).** Loist is literally an MCP server. Instead of a destination app competing with Vistex/Synchtank, be the **agent-accessible workflow layer on top of the ERPs and DISCO** — "clearance agents" that read/write incumbent systems, chase counterparties by email, and maintain the LOI-39 task state machine. Sells as automation ROI (hours saved per clearance), not seats; rides the 2025–26 enterprise-agent budget wave; avoids rip-and-replace.
2. **Indie publishers + sync agencies first, majors never (initially).** 200–400 companies reachable by founder-led sales, $5–25k ACVs, 1–3-month cycles, no procurement gauntlet. Small but honest; wedge to expand from.
3. **Supervisor-side first** — attractive workflow logic (supervisors originate clearances) but the population is tiny (~hundreds to ~1,500) and DISCO already owns them; only viable as a partnership, not a beachhead.
4. **Clearance-status network ("the DocuSign of sync")** — per-transaction pricing on confirmed clearances; highest upside, hardest cold start; do only after #1 or #2 earns trust.

## Bottom line
**The thesis as stated — Linear-style seats sold to the biggest 20% of music publishing — fails on arithmetic and on go-to-market fit.** The market is ~$8B of *revenue* but only thousands of *seats*; the top of it is locked by 40-year incumbents and guarded by procurement processes a pre-auth, single-tenant, solo-dev product cannot enter. The defensible version of the idea is a **pivot: an agentic/MCP clearance-workflow layer sold to independent publishers and sync agencies first**, priced per catalog or per outcome, with majors as a year-3 ambition only after SOC 2, multi-tenancy, and 5+ referenceable design partners exist.
